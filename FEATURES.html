Part one · What your customer sees

The storefront

The half of the website that turns a visitor into an order. Everything here works on a phone as well as a laptop.

Sections for who you sell to Group 1 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Menswear / Womenswear / Kidswearrare A visitor is asked once which section they want. Categories, homepage, search and footer all change to match. Sell to men and women without the site feeling confused. One website does the job of two, and if you sell to only one audience the question never appears.
  • Separate categories per section Sherwani, kurta pyjama, indo-western, suiting for men. Bridal, sarees, lehengas, gowns for women. Kids has its own. A man shopping for a sherwani never scrolls past lehengas, so he finds what he came for and does not leave.
  • Its own homepage per section Different banner, headline and bestsellers for each section. The first screen already speaks to the person who landed on it, instead of asking them to filter.
  • Universal items A stole or dupatta with no section set shows to everyone. Accessories keep selling to all three sections without you listing them three times.

Finding a product Group 2 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Search as you type Results appear from the second letter — products, categories and fabrics. A customer who knows what they want reaches it in two seconds instead of giving up on your menu.
  • Filters with no page reload Colour, fabric, size, occasion, price. The grid fades and updates; the web address updates too. Browsing feels instant, and a customer can send a filtered view — "red lehengas under ₹50,000" — straight to her sister on WhatsApp.
  • Counts on every filter "Raw Silk (12)" next to each option. Nobody clicks into an empty result and concludes your shop has nothing.
  • Sort five ways Featured, newest, popularity, price low to high, high to low. Budget shoppers and premium shoppers both get to the right end of your range quickly.
  • Endless scroll and Load more Both, on the same page. More of your catalogue actually gets seen, which matters most for the pieces sitting on page two.
  • Category dropdown menus Full-width menus showing featured pieces, not a plain text list. Your best stock is visible from every page of the site.
  • Wishlist and quick view Save for later; open a product without leaving the grid. A bride comparing eight lehengas can shortlist instead of losing her place — and comes back to the list.

The product page Group 3 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Four photos visible at once Two-by-two on a laptop, swipeable on a phone, magnify on hover. Embroidery and fabric detail sell themselves. Fewer "can you send more photos on WhatsApp" messages for your staff to answer.
  • Colour and size on every product With actual colour swatches, not just names. Fewer wrong-colour orders, which means fewer returns you pay courier both ways for.
  • Pincode check before the cartrare Six digits and the customer sees the delivery date, whether you deliver there yourself, and whether COD is available. The two questions that stop a purchase are answered before the customer commits — instead of surfacing at checkout, where they abandon.
  • Price with nothing hidden "Inclusive of all taxes" plus the delivery position stated right on the product page. No surprise charge at checkout. Surprise charges are the single biggest reason a full cart is abandoned.
  • Buy buttons that follow the pagerare A slim bar keeps Add to bag and Buy now on screen while scrolling, and disappears once they browse other pieces. A customer who has decided at the bottom of the page does not have to scroll back up to buy.
  • Size guide per audience Chest and shoulder for men, bust and hip for women, on tabs. A man is not shown a bust measurement. Right size chosen first time means fewer size-exchange returns.
  • Specifications, care, questions Fabric, occasion, SKU, wash care, and a FAQ per product. Answers the questions your team currently answers on the phone, at 11pm, one customer at a time.
  • Related pieces From the same section, closest in price. A bigger average order — someone buying a sherwani sees the stole that goes with it.

Reviews from real buyers Group 4 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Only customers can reviewrare A review needs the order number plus the phone or email on that order, and the order must be delivered. A competitor cannot post a fake one-star review on your bestseller. Your ratings stay worth something.
  • Photos and videos in reviews Uploads start compressing while the customer is still typing. A real customer photo of your lehenga sells better than any studio shot, and nobody waits for an upload bar.
  • You approve before it appears Nothing publishes until you have read it. A genuine complaint reaches you privately first, so you can fix it instead of reading it in public.
  • Live Google reviews Your Google rating can be pulled onto the site. Reviews you have already earned start working on your own website too.

Bag and checkout Group 5 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Slide-out bag Change quantity without leaving the page. The bag survives closing the browser for 30 days. Someone who was interrupted comes back a week later and their bag is still there.
  • Checkout without an account Three steps on one page: address, delivery, payment. No "create a password" step. Forcing account creation loses a meaningful share of first-time buyers.
  • Pincode fills city and staterare Six digits and the address completes itself, with local area suggestions. Three fewer fields to type on a phone, and far fewer wrong addresses — which means fewer failed deliveries you pay for.
  • Full price breakdown Items, discount, delivery with the waived charge struck through, total payable, GST disclosed, and "you save ₹X". The customer can see they are getting a deal, and can see nothing is hidden. Both raise the chance they finish.
  • Total updates as they choose Pick express or gift wrap and the total changes instantly. Nobody is surprised by a charge they picked, so express delivery actually gets bought instead of questioned.
  • Coupon codes Percentage, flat amount or free shipping, with minimum order, expiry and usage limits. Run a Diwali sale or an influencer code without giving away more than you planned.
  • GST number for business buyersrare A reseller enters their GSTIN and gets a proper B2B invoice. The number is checked digit-by-digit. Boutiques and resellers can buy from you, because they can claim their input credit. That is a whole customer segment most small sites cannot serve.
  • Stock re-checked at payment If someone else bought the last piece while the bag sat open, nothing is charged. You never take money for something you cannot send — which is the worst call your team can have to make.

Payment and cash on delivery Group 6 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Razorpay, on your own account UPI, cards, net banking, EMI. Your keys, your bank. Money settles directly to you. Nobody sits between you and your revenue.
  • Partial cash on deliveryrare Take 25% online, the rest at the door. Percentage or flat, your choice. The single biggest lever on returns. On a ₹90,000 sherwani, a customer who has paid ₹22,500 does not refuse the parcel — and you are not paying courier both ways for nothing.
  • COD off by pincode Block specific pincodes or whole ranges — or invert it and allow COD only where you trust it. Set a maximum order value. The areas that cost you money stop costing you money, without switching COD off for everyone.
  • Payment verified before the order exists An abandoned payment never creates an order. Your order list stays real. No phantom orders to chase, and your sales figures are not inflated.

Delivery, including your own Group 7 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Same-day delivery in your own cityrare Deliver across your city yourself, same evening, free — courier for everyone else. With time slots. You keep the courier fee, the customer gets it today, and you can promise something no marketplace can. For a local shop this is the strongest thing on your website.
  • Orders marked "we deliver this" The order says plainly: do not book a courier. Nobody in your team pays for a courier on a parcel you already delivered by hand.
  • Store pickup Customer collects from the shop. Zero delivery cost, and they walk into your shop — where they buy more.
  • Honest cut-off times Same-day stops being offered after 2pm. You never break a promise the website made on your behalf at 6pm.
  • Delivery charges by method Free above an amount you set; express and nominated-date at your own rates. Free shipping pushes up your average order value, and express becomes a small extra income instead of a cost.

After the order Group 8 of 8

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain

  • Track an order without an account Order number plus phone or email. "Where is my order?" stops being a phone call to your staff.

Part two · What you operate

The admin panel

Twenty-five screens. This half decides whether running the shop takes an hour a day or an afternoon — and it is where cheap websites are usually hollow.

What you actually earned Group 1 of 7

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Profit in hand, not just salesrare Sales, minus refunds, GST, cost of goods, packaging, courier, COD handling, gateway fee, returns provision, marketing, rent, salaries — then depreciation, loan interest and income tax. You find out on the 3rd of the month whether last month made money. Most shop owners find out from their CA in July.
  • EBITDA and take-home shown separately Two different numbers, both on screen. You can see the business is healthy while your own take-home is thin — and know it is the loan, not the shop.
  • Your cost per item, hidden from customers Enter what each piece costs you. Never shown on the storefront. Without this, no profit figure on any website is real. With it, you know a ₹500 sale on a ₹100 piece nets you ₹376.
  • Nine time windows, each compared Today, 2, 5, 7, 15, 30, 90 days, 12 months, all time — or any two dates. Each compared with the same length before it. You see whether this week beat last week without doing arithmetic.
  • Did that influencer post work?rare Short windows plus campaign tracking on every order. Two days after a paid post you know exactly what it sold. You stop paying the influencers who do not sell.
  • Profit per product and per category Margin, units and profit for every SKU. You reorder what makes money instead of what looks busy. Some bestsellers lose money and only this screen shows it.
  • Coupon economics What each code cost you, and how much came back as returns. Coupon orders are numbered separately. You find out that your discount code buyers return twice as often — before you run the sale again.
  • Dead stock and low stock What has not sold, and what is running out. Cash stuck on a shelf gets cleared, and you stop losing sales to items quietly at zero.

GST, done properly Group 2 of 7

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Proper tax invoices One unbroken series per financial year, allocated once, never reused. A gap or a duplicate in the series is the first thing an officer asks about. There is nothing to explain.
  • CGST/SGST or IGST decided correctlyrare Same state as you: CGST plus SGST. Different state: IGST. Taken from the buyer's address. Get this wrong on a ₹1,50,000 order and you either underpay tax or overcharge a customer. Neither is cheap to unwind.
  • Rate by price slab Garments under ₹2,500 at 5%, at or above at 18% — per piece, not per order. Correct tax on a mixed basket without you checking each line.
  • HSN code on every line Per product, with a store-wide fallback. Your invoices and your returns match, which is what an audit looks for.
  • Papers your CA can file fromrare GSTR-1 tables ready-made: B2CS, B2CL, B2B, HSN summary, documents issued, credit notes, and the 3B figure — and the tables reconcile with each other. Your CA's monthly job becomes minutes, not a day of reading order screenshots. Ask your CA what they currently charge for that.
  • TDS and TCS treated as recoverable What a marketplace withholds is reported as money you claim back, never as a cost. Your profit figure is not quietly understated by 2% of your marketplace sales.

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain

  • Export any date range A week, a month, a custom range, as a spreadsheet. Send your CA exactly the period they asked for, not the whole year.
  • Automatic emails on status change Shipped and delivered notify the customer. Your staff stop sending status updates by hand.

Products Group 4 of 7

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain

  • Photos and video compressed automaticallyrare Every upload becomes WebP; videos are re-encoded and given a poster frame. A 4 MB phone photo becomes a few hundred KB. Your site stays fast as the catalogue grows — and a slow site is a site that does not sell.

Returns and refunds Group 5 of 7

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Refund part of an order Not just all or nothing. Several returns on one order add up correctly. One item back out of three is handled properly, so your books stay right.
  • Refunds move your profit figure A refund is not just a note on the order. Your month's profit is true. A shop that counts refunded orders as sales does not know how it is doing.
  • Refund through Razorpay One click. If the gateway refuses, you are told to do it by hand. Refunds take a minute, and the system never pretends a failed refund succeeded.
  • Return window you set Counted from delivery, not from the order date. A customer whose parcel took a week does not lose a week of their return window — and nobody argues about the dates.

  • Sitemap and structured data Prices, ratings and stock published in the format Google reads. Your listing shows a price and stars instead of a plain blue link, which is a large difference in clicks.

Your data, your staff, your safety Group 7 of 7

FeatureWhat it isWhat you gain
  • Download everything, any timerare One button: orders, customers, products, reviews, returns — as spreadsheets and data files. Imports into Shopify or WooCommerce. You are never trapped. If you ever want to leave, you leave with everything, without asking permission.
  • Staff logins with limits Owner, manager, staff. Enforced on the server, not by hiding menu items. Your packing help processes orders and returns and never sees your costs, your profit or your settings.
  • Real login security Passwords stored so nobody can read them, locked after repeated wrong attempts, reset links usable once. Your customer list and your margins are not one guessed password away.
  • Backup before every change Every edit takes a timestamped copy first, plus a one-command full backup. A wrong bulk upload is undone in a minute instead of retyping your catalogue.
  • Log of who did what Every price change, refund and setting, with a name and a time. When a price is wrong or a refund is unexplained, you know who and when — no guessing.
  • Pre-launch check One command that refuses to go live with a test payment key, a placeholder brand or a missing GST number. Your shop does not open with test payments switched on, which is a mistake that happens more often than anyone admits.

What is honestly not here yet

No customer accounts — checkout is guest-only and orders are looked up by number and phone. No automatic courier booking yet, though the groundwork is done. No abandoned-cart reminders. The GST papers are working papers your CA files from, not a one-click portal upload.

These are on the roadmap and will be told to you before you buy, not after. Any vendor who says their software has no limitations has not tested it.

Part three · Take this with you

Fourteen questions for the other quote

Do not compare prices — compare answers. Ask these in order and write down what you hear. Each takes an honest vendor half a minute, and anyone else a long time.

  1. Does my invoice split CGST and SGST for my own state, and switch to IGST for other states, automatically?This is the law, not a feature. Ask to see a real invoice PDF for an out-of-state order.
  2. Are my invoice numbers one unbroken series per financial year?A gap or duplicate is the first thing a GST officer questions. Ask what happens when an order is cancelled.
  3. Can my CA file GSTR-1 from your admin, or will they be typing from order screenshots?This one question separates a real system from a website with a payment button.
  4. Can I enter what each item costs me, and see profit after GST, courier, packaging and gateway fees?Most cheap websites show revenue and call it profit. Ask to see the actual screen.
  5. Can I take 25% now and the rest as cash on delivery?On a ₹90,000 order this is the difference between a sale and a returned parcel.
  6. Can I switch off cash on delivery for specific pincodes?Every ethnic-wear shop has a list of areas that cost them money.
  7. Can I deliver in my own city myself and use a courier for the rest?And will the order tell my staff not to book a courier for it?
  8. Can a customer check delivery date and COD by pincode before adding to the bag?Answering that after checkout has started is where the sale is lost.
  9. Who can post a review?If anyone can, your competitor can. Ask whether a review requires a delivered order.
  10. Are customer photos and videos compressed automatically?If not, your site gets slower every month and nobody notices until sales drop.
  11. Can I give my packing staff a login that hides my costs and profit?One shared owner password is not a permission system.
  12. Can I download all my data — orders, customers, products — right now, without asking you?This is the lock-in question. A vendor who hesitates has already answered it.
  13. What does this cost per year after year one, including every app I will need?Get COD, WhatsApp, reviews, GST invoicing and a page builder priced individually. On a hosted platform the apps often cost more per year than the build did.
  14. If you stop answering the phone in a year, what do I have?Ask for it in writing: what you hold, what happens to your data, who else can maintain it.

The one number to work out before you decide

Add up the yearly cost, not the build cost. Platform fee, plus a COD app, plus WhatsApp, plus reviews, plus GST invoicing, plus a page builder — charged every month, usually rising as you sell more.

Then ask what a wrong tax split on a ₹1,50,000 order costs, or one Saturday offline in wedding season. A ₹15,000 website is not expensive. It is cheap for what it is — and that is a different sentence.
